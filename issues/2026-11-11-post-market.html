How the floors were built ESTIMATE

Classic pivots use Wednesday's official high (25,424.35), low (25,095.35) and close (25,255.05). [2]

  • P = (H + L + C) ÷ 3 = 25,258.25
  • R1 = 2P − L = 25,421.15; S1 = 2P − H = 25,092.15
  • R2 = P + (H − L) = 25,587.25; S2 = P − (H − L) = 24,929.25

Notice the neat coincidences: the close sits almost on the pivot, the high almost on R1 and the low almost on S1. Analyst zones are ranges, so they overlap the calculated floors. [4] [5]

Levels are lifeguard flags, Captain, not walls. The sea can still wash past them.

Deep Dive · What powered the wave?

Crude and the rupee FACT

Brent's January futures contract traded as low as US$90.75 a barrel during India's session, versus a previous close of US$92.08, as hopes of US–Iran de-escalation cooled the oil scare. Cheaper oil trims India's import bill. The rupee closed near 93.69 per US dollar, about 0.2% firmer and touching its strongest level since April, helped by foreign buying of Indian shares. [1] [8] [10]

Gold and the global tide FACT

Spot gold traded near US$4,058 an ounce (up about 1%) and MCX December gold above ₹1,41,770 per 10 grams at 1:44 pm IST. Overseas, US core inflation rose 0.3% in October versus 0.2% expected, so traders now price a December Fed rate increase. Wall Street dipped on Tuesday, while Asia was mixed: Kospi rose 2.53% and the Hang Seng fell 1.22%. [9] [7] [1]

The FII/DII tide chart FACT

Wednesday's provisional FII/DII cash figures were not yet available at cutoff. The latest dated figures are for Monday 9 Nov: foreign institutions net bought ₹7,212.22 crore and domestic institutions net sold ₹7,172.53 crore. Exchange data show FPIs bought a net ₹56,771 crore over the eight sessions to 9 Nov, the biggest such streak since December 2020, as global funds rotated away from AI-heavy markets. [1] [11]

Breadth and heavyweights FACT

This was a broad wave. On the NSE, 2,495 stocks rose and 1,166 fell (106 unchanged, 3,767 traded): roughly two risers per faller. Smallcap 100 (+1.59%) outran the Nifty. Mahindra & Mahindra (+5.30%) and InterGlobe Aviation (+4.33%) led, and PSU banks rallied 2.94%. The main drags were ICICI Bank (−1.10%), a heavyweight, and Adani Ports (−1.84%). [1]

Our call for the next session

Paddling in the shallows: a range day with a slight upward lean OPINION

Most probable path: Nifty spends Thursday inside Wednesday's 25,095–25,424 range, rotating around the 25,258 pivot, with buyers stepping in on dips but no clean break above 25,424 before India's CPI number.

Why: Foreign money has poured in for eight straight sessions, breadth was strong (about two risers per faller) and banks are leading. But momentum is overbought, the index faded 169 points from its high, VIX ticked up, and a likely above-6% CPI print after the close gives traders a reason to wait.

What pushes against it: A fresh oil spike, a hawkish turn in US yields, or a pause in foreign buying could tip it into a deeper pullback. A surprise headline on US–Iran talks could do the opposite and launch a breakout.

Decision zone: 25,200–25,300, around the pivot and Wednesday's close.

We are wrong if Nifty closes Thursday above 25,424 (a breakout through the crest) or below 25,092 (a break of Wednesday's low and S1).
